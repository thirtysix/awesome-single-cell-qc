# Awesome Single-Cell QC

A comprehensive catalog of single-cell QC and preprocessing tools, methods and evidence, with an emphasis on **salvaging cells and signal in multiplexed experiments**, plus a map of the adjacent concepts that QC choices feed into.

**291 records** (235 core, 56 adjacent) and 58 benchmark studies, built 2026-10-26. Every entry carries a tier, the side it works on (keeping cells, removing them, or either), what it needs as input, and, where it recovers cells or signal, the reported gain and who measured it.

**Browse it at https://thirtysix.github.io/awesome-single-cell-qc/**: the full catalog, with filters for layer, category, kind, tier, side and required input, and the review chapters. The same page is `docs/index.html` (`make serve`, then http://127.0.0.1:8510/). On GitHub, [docs/records.md](docs/records.md) lists every record.

### Hashing demultiplexing

*Assigning cells to samples from hashtag, lipid or CMO tags; tag-count models*

- **[Cell Hashing (antibody hashtags)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-018-1603-1)** `standard`: Oligo-tagged antibodies against ubiquitous surface proteins label each sample before pooling, so samples share one lane and cross-sample multiplets become identifiable
  <sub>Recovers: cells per lane through superloading, and doublets that RNA-only methods miss · Gain: 8-donor PBMC pool superloaded for about 20,000 singlets: modelled unresolved multiplet rate 3.1%, against about 4,000 singlets at the same rate without hashing (described as about 400% more cells for the same cost); observed 14,002 singlets and 2,974 identifiable multiplets (self-reported) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-018-1603-1) · `1,045 cites`</sub>
- **[HTODemux (Seurat)](https://satijalab.org/seurat/articles/hashing_vignette)** `standard`: Seurat function that CLR-normalises hashtag counts, clusters droplets with k-medoids, fits a negative binomial to each tag's lowest cluster and calls the tag positive above that background's 0.99 quantile
  <sub>Recovers: sample labels for hashed cells; flags cross-sample doublets and tagless negatives · Gain: Lowering positive.quantile from 0.99 to 0.9 raised overall classification accuracy from 0.54 to 0.65 on antibody-hashed nuclei of four cell lines (Mylka 2022); mean F 0.892, 0.794 and 0.742 on three BAL batches against vireo genotype truth, with under 20% of genetic doublets called singlets (Howitt 2023) (independent) · [code](https://github.com/satijalab/seurat) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-018-1603-1) · `1,045 cites | 2,807 stars`</sub>
- **[HashSolo](https://scanpy.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/scanpy.external.pp.hashsolo.html)** `standard`: Bayesian hashing demultiplexer in scanpy (sc.external.pp.hashsolo) that models signal and noise tag counts and scores each droplet's top tags under negative, singlet and doublet hypotheses with user priors
  <sub>Recovers: singlet calls with few negatives · Gain: Howitt 2023 (vireo truth): mean F 0.920, 0.783 and 0.737 on three BAL batches and fewer negatives than any caller except BFFcluster, but about 30% (BAL) and 60% (cell lines) of genetic doublets were called singlets (independent) · [code](https://github.com/calico/solo) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2020.05.010) · `280 cites | 98 stars | archived`</sub>
- **[hashedDrops (DropletUtils)](https://bioconductor.org/packages/DropletUtils)** `standard`: DropletUtils function that removes ambient tag levels and calls singlets and doublets from the log-fold change between a droplet's top and second tag, with MAD-based outlier thresholds
  <sub>Recovers: more singlets when its confidence threshold is lowered · Gain: With confident.min lowered from 2 to 0.5 it had the highest mean F in 4 of 6 batches in Howitt 2023 (BAL batch 2: 0.847 versus 0.613 at the default), at the cost of roughly doubling genetic doublets called singlets (about 3 000 extra in BAL batch 3) (independent) · [code](https://github.com/MarioniLab/DropletUtils) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.18129/B9.bioc.DropletUtils) · `68 stars`</sub>
- **[demuxEM](https://github.com/lilab-bcb/demuxEM)** `benchmarked-sota`: Hashing demultiplexer built for nucleus hashing that estimates each tag's background from empty droplets and uses EM to split a droplet's tag counts into background and true signal before calling
  <sub>Recovers: singlets from noisy nucleus hashing · Gain: Self-reported on hashed brain nuclei: 96% agreement with demuxlet versus 92% for Seurat, 94.8% agreement with Xist-based sex. In hadge 2024 it matched vireo donors best together with MULTI-seq on hashed brain nuclei (one dataset) (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/lilab-bcb/demuxEM) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-10756-2) · `189 cites | 2 stars`</sub>
- **[GMM-Demux](https://github.com/CHPGenetics/GMM-demux)** `benchmarked-sota`: Command-line tool that fits a two-component Gaussian mixture to each CLR-normalised tag and models droplet formation to estimate multiplet rates and flag multiplet-induced (phony) clusters
  <sub>Recovers: singlet calls with confidence scores; an estimate of undetectable same-sample multiplets for planning · Gain: Howitt 2023: mean F 0.905, 0.770, 0.720 (BAL) and 0.823 (cell lines), under 20% of genetic doublets called singlets, judged close to demuxmix and HTODemux. Self-reported: PBMC multiplet rate cut from 23.9% to 6.45%, then 3.29% after removing 9 phony clusters (independent) · [code](https://github.com/CHPGenetics/GMM-demux) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-020-02084-2) · `74 cites | 19 stars`</sub>
- **[deMULTIplex2](https://github.com/Gartner-Lab/deMULTIplex2)** `benchmarked-sota`: R package that models tag cross-contamination from its physical mechanism with two negative binomial GLMs (tag-count and cosine-similarity spaces) fitted by EM, giving each cell a posterior sample probability
  <sub>Recovers: singlets from noisy, imbalanced or low-cell samples, including samples whose tag counts are not bimodal · Gain: Self-reported: 63.2% versus 50.8% of all cells correctly retrieved (deMULTIplex) on multiplexed breast-cancer PDX, including two samples other callers missed; over 99% recall versus 52% (HTODemux) and 73% (demuxmix naive) in a 30-sample imbalanced simulation; accuracy held with about 10 cells per sample and with 5% of tag reads (median 30 tags per cell) on clean data (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/Gartner-Lab/deMULTIplex2) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-024-03177-y) · `34 cites, 68 across 3 papers | 21 stars`</sub>
- **[demuxmix](https://bioconductor.org/packages/demuxmix)** `benchmarked-sota`: Bioconductor package that fits negative binomial regression mixture models to raw tag counts, optionally with each droplet's detected-gene count as a covariate, and returns a posterior error probability for every assignment
  <sub>Recovers: weakly tagged singlets with normal RNA; supports pooling one untagged sample with tagged ones · Gain: Best overall of 7 callers in Howitt 2023 (mean F 0.907, 0.833, 0.775 on BAL; under 20% of genetic doublets called singlets). Self-reported: 94% of singlets classified versus 87-93% for other callers (brain nuclei) and 85% versus 67-81% (cell-line mixture); untagged CSF cells pooled with tagged PBMC separated at sensitivity 0.980 and specificity 0.985 (independent) · [code](https://github.com/huklein/demuxmix) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btad481) · `30 cites, 61 across 2 papers | 5 stars`</sub>

### Genotype demultiplexing

*Assigning cells to donors or cell lines from SNPs; hybrids with hashing*

- **[demuxlet / freemuxlet (popscle)](https://github.com/statgen/popscle)** `standard`: Reference-genotype demultiplexer (popscle suite, with freemuxlet as its genotype-free mode) that scores each droplet's SNP reads against every donor and donor pair
  <sub>Recovers: donor labels and between-donor doublets from SNP reads · Gain: Self-reported: in simulation 50 SNPs per cell assigned 97% of singlets and detected 92% of doublets in pools of up to 64; with genotypes for 8 pooled samples over 99% of singlets were assigned correctly (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/statgen/popscle) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.4042) · `1,143 cites | 59 stars`</sub>
- **[souporcell](https://github.com/wheaton5/souporcell)** `benchmarked-sota`: Genotype-free demultiplexer that calls variants from the pooled reads, clusters cells by allele counts with an explicit ambient-RNA term, and flags cross-genotype doublets
  <sub>Recovers: donor or cell-line labels for every cell with enough variant reads, independent of tags; an ambient RNA estimate · Gain: Independent (Demuxafy): with vireo, unaffected by simulated ambient RNA in pools of up to 16 donors, but performance drops above 32 donors. Self-reported: 6 612 of 6 622 singlets and 415 of 451 doublets correct in a synthetic mixture; a minority sample resolved down to 0.6% of cells (independent) · [code](https://github.com/wheaton5/souporcell) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-0820-1) · `502 cites, 505 across 2 papers | 215 stars`</sub>
- **[vireo (with cellSNP-lite)](https://vireosnp.readthedocs.io)** `benchmarked-sota`: Bayesian variational model that assigns cells to donors from allele counts (cellSNP-lite pileups) with full, partial or no reference genotypes and calls between-donor doublets
  <sub>Recovers: donor or cell-line labels independent of tags; doublets between genotypes · Gain: Most accurate and fastest of vireo, souporcell, freemuxlet and scSplit on simulated snRNA-seq pools (Fu 2025); best single method without reference genotypes at 16 or more donors (Demuxafy); on pooled tumours, 99.0-99.9% singlet recall with bulk RNA-seq genotypes and 99.2% of its doublet calls true versus 31.9% for demuxlet (Weber 2021) (independent) · [code](https://github.com/single-cell-genetics/vireo) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-019-1865-2) · `344 cites | 105 stars`</sub>
- **[Demuxalot](https://github.com/arogozhnikov/demuxalot)** `benchmarked-sota`: Reference-genotype demultiplexer that refines donor genotypes from the pooled reads before assigning cells, built for large pools
  <sub>Recovers: donor labels with the lowest error among genotype tools in Demuxafy · Gain: Independent (Demuxafy): Demuxalot (refined) had the fewest wrong droplets, about 1% at 2 donors and 3% at 16 or more, and the best detection of between-donor doublets; combined with Dropulation it raised MCC by about 0.09 over single methods (independent) · [code](https://github.com/arogozhnikov/demuxalot) · [preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.05.22.443646) · `18 cites | 27 stars`</sub>
- **[MIX-Seq](https://github.com/broadinstitute/mix_seq_ms)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Pools of 24-99 cancer cell lines are co-treated in one culture and each cell is assigned to its line from transcribed SNPs, with Cell Hashing layered on for time points or doses
  <sub>Recovers: cell lines and conditions per lane without per-line tags; empty droplets and doublets recognised as SNP mixtures · Gain: 99-line trametinib pool: average 130 cells per line per condition, 97 of 99 lines with at least 20 cells; 0 of 84,869 cells assigned to an out-of-pool line from a 494-line reference; classification robust with 50-100 detected SNP sites; downsampling suggested tens of cells per condition suffice for a per-line response profile; up to 40,000 cells loaded per 10x channel (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/broadinstitute/mix_seq_ms) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-17440-w) · `172 cites | 16 stars`</sub>
- **[Demuxafy](https://demultiplexing-doublet-detecting-docs.readthedocs.io)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Containerised framework that runs 7 genotype demultiplexers and 7 transcriptional doublet detectors, combines them by intersection, and recommends combinations by pool design
  <sub>Recovers: more correctly classified singlets and doublets than any single method · Gain: Self-reported on 74 PBMC pools (12-16 donors, ~20 000 droplets), fibroblast pools and 70 in silico pools of 2-128 donors: recommended combinations raised MCC by about 0.09 over single methods (FDR < 0.05); demultiplexing alone leaves 173 (16 donors) to 1 325 (2 donors) same-donor, different-cell-type doublets per 20 000-droplet capture (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/drneavin/Demultiplexing_Doublet_Detecting_Docs) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-024-03224-8) · `87 cites | 20 stars`</sub>
- **[Weber et al. 2021 genetic demultiplexing in cancer](https://github.com/lmweber/snp-dmx-cancer)** `published-unbenchmarked`: In silico benchmark of genetic demultiplexing on pooled tumour scRNA-seq (high copy-number ovarian cancer, high mutation-burden lung adenocarcinoma) with a reusable Snakemake workflow
  <sub>Gain: cellSNP/vireo with bulk RNA-seq genotypes recalled 99.0-99.9% of singlets (precision 100%, 85.9%, 77.4% at 0, 20, 30% doublets); 1000 Genomes reference recall 97.9-99.0%; 99.2% of vireo doublet calls were true versus 31.9% for demuxlet (independent) · [code](https://github.com/lmweber/snp-dmx-cancer) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giab062) · `37 cites | 3 stars`</sub>
- **[HTOreader and hybrid hashing-genotype demultiplexing](https://github.com/WilsonImmunologyLab/HTOreader)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Hashing caller whose cut-offs resist many tags and unequal sample sizes, plus a hybrid strategy that names souporcell genotype clusters by their dominant hashtag and labels every cell that either method calls a singlet
  <sub>Recovers: hashing negatives and false hashing doublets in genetically distinct pools; one unhashed sample, or unstained aliquots of hashed donors · Gain: Self-reported, 8-donor B and T cell pool: the hybrid added 5.2% singlets, mostly cells hashing had called doublets (715) or negatives (166); 88.03% of cells (13 777 of 15 650) assigned across a hashed and an unhashed pool sharing donors; about 100 hashtagged cells per donor sufficed to name clusters (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/WilsonImmunologyLab/HTOreader) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbae254) · `11 cites, 22 across 2 papers | 3 stars`</sub>

### Combinatorial indexing & split-pool

*sci-, SPLiT-, Parse-style barcoding and nuclear hashing; their calling and collisions*

- **[SPLiT-seq / Parse Evercode](https://www.parsebiosciences.com)** `benchmarked`: Fixed cells or nuclei are barcoded in situ through rounds of split-pool ligation in plates, so the first-round well doubles as a sample barcode without hashing
  <sub>Recovers: samples per run (96-384 per commercial kit, vendor-reported) and storage of fixed cells; fewer stressed cells than unfixed droplet capture in one organoid comparison · Gain: Independent: about 2x genes per cell against 10x 3' on mouse thymocytes (Filippov 2024); post-QC retention 95.6% against 87.2% and fewer stressed cells than 10x CellPlex on cerebellar organoids (Sarieva 2026); self-reported: 0.1% mouse-human collisions and about $0.01 per cell library preparation (2018) (independent) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aam8999) · `1,422 cites, 1,473 across 4 papers`</sub>
- **[sci-RNA-seq3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-0969-x)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Three-level single-cell combinatorial indexing of fixed nuclei (reverse-transcription, ligation and PCR barcodes) that scales exponentially with plate size
  <sub>Recovers: cells per experiment at a reagent cost near one cent per cell; reverse-transcription wells act as sample barcodes · Gain: About 2 million nuclei from 61 mouse embryos in one experiment (2019); optimised protocol (Martin 2023): reagent cost about 1 cent per cell or less, DSP/methanol fixation gives more UMIs than paraformaldehyde, and new buffers reduce nuclei loss during washes (self-reported) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-0969-x) · `4,465 cites, 4,598 across 2 papers`</sub>
- **[sci-Plex](https://github.com/cole-trapnell-lab/sci-plex)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Nuclear hashing with unmodified polyadenylated oligos combined with sci-RNA-seq3 combinatorial indexing, used to read thousands of independently treated wells in one experiment
  <sub>Recovers: conditions per experiment, collision (doublet) flags from well hashes, and cell number per well as a viability readout · Gain: 4,992 independently treated wells (188 compounds x 4 doses x 3 cell lines, in duplicate) and 649,340 cells in one experiment; median 100-200 cells per condition; replicate-well pseudobulk Pearson r = 0.99; hashes flagged 95.5% of interspecies collisions at collision rates of 7-23% (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/cole-trapnell-lab/sci-plex) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax6234) · `398 cites | 40 stars`</sub>
- **[scifi-RNA-seq](https://github.com/epigen/scifiRNA-seq)** `published-unbenchmarked`: One round of in-cell preindexing followed by deliberately overloaded droplets, so several cells per droplet are resolved by the pair of preindex and droplet barcodes
  <sub>Recovers: cells per channel: transcriptomes from multi-cell droplets are kept instead of discarded as doublets · Gain: Up to 150,000 cells per Chromium channel and over 1 million per chip; 100-fold overloading kept a stable emulsion; recovered transcriptomes scaled linearly from 15,300 to 765,000 loaded nuclei; no loss of complexity in droplets holding up to 15 nuclei (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/epigen/scifiRNA-seq) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-021-01153-z) · `270 cites | 13 stars`</sub>
- **[UDA-seq](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02586-y)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Overloaded droplets are barcoded first and a plate-based post-indexing round then splits each droplet barcode into single cells, across RNA, ATAC and multiome modalities
  <sub>Recovers: cells and samples per channel at a low collision rate · Gain: Collision rate 1.23% with 96-well post-indexing against 6.29% expected from the droplet barcode alone; over 100,000 cells from three dozen frozen clinical biopsies in one channel (self-reported) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02586-y) · `54 cites`</sub>
- **[Input-to-output recovery, split-pool versus droplet](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25073828)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Independent comparisons that report cells recovered relative to cells put in for Parse split-pool barcoding and 10x droplet capture
  <sub>Gain: PBMCs (Xie 2024): about 53% of input cells recovered with 10x against about 27% with Parse, valid-barcode reads about 98% against 85%; cerebellar organoids (Sarieva 2026): 42.7% against 16.5%, with post-QC retention 87.2% against 95.6% (independent) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25073828) · `34 cites, 51 across 3 papers`</sub>
- **[Kuijpers et al. 2024 SPLiT-seq pipeline comparison](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-024-10285-3)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Comparison of eight SPLiT-seq processing pipelines (alevin-fry splitp, LR-splitpipe, SCSit, splitpipe, splitpipeline, SPLiTseq-demultiplex, STARsolo, zUMIs) for speed, barcode calls and downstream results
  <sub>Gain: STARsolo, splitpipe and alevin-fry splitp handled large data in reasonable time; STARsolo was nearly identical to splitpipe; recommends splitpipe or STARsolo (independent) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-024-10285-3) · `27 cites`</sub>
- **[sci-Plex-GxE](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2023.100487)** `published-unbenchmarked`: sci-Plex extended with CROP-seq guide capture so drug, dose and CRISPR perturbation are read from the same nucleus, with two independent transductions per condition
  <sub>Recovers: true replicates (independent transductions) and hash-based doublet removal in a gene-by-drug grid · Gain: 1,052,205 cells over 28,242 conditions (14,121 gene-by-environment combinations x 2 independent transductions); 94.3% of cells assigned to a condition after doublet removal; about 35 cells per condition on average (calculated from the reported totals); pilot: 94.4% of 18,585 cells assigned to a well (self-reported) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2023.100487) · `22 cites`</sub>

### Alignment & quantification

*Read alignment, barcode and UMI correction, count matrices; the choices that set what cell calling sees*

- **[Cell Ranger (cellranger count / multi)](https://www.10xgenomics.com/support/software/cell-ranger)** `standard`: 10x Genomics pipeline that aligns reads with STAR, corrects barcodes and UMIs against the kit whitelist, counts UMIs per gene and calls cells, producing the raw and filtered matrices most 10x analyses start from
  <sub>Recovers: a reproducible, vendor-supported count matrix; low-RNA cells through its EmptyDrops-style second calling step · Gain: You 2021 (10 workflows, mixtures with known labels): performance "consistently high and relatively stable", among the top 3 on one downstream ranking; Bruning 2022: gene sets similar to STARsolo and alevin-fry (independent) · [code](https://github.com/10XGenomics/cellranger) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms14049) · `7,329 cites, 7,459 across 4 papers | 481 stars`</sub>
- **[Counting intronic (nascent) reads, especially for nuclei](https://doi.org/10.1093/biostatistics/kxad010)** `standard`: Counting reads that fall in introns towards the gene (Cell Ranger include-introns, default from v7.0; STARsolo GeneFull; alevin-fry splici/USA mode; kb nac index) instead of discarding them
  <sub>Recovers: library size and genes in single-nucleus data, where much of the RNA is unspliced; cell types that exon-only counting blurs · Gain: Kuo 2024 (independent, 10x mouse cortex nuclei): exon-only references gave significantly smaller library sizes and discordant SingleR labels for astrocytes vs neural stem cells; Pool 2023: intronic read handling is one of three causes of missing genes in droplet data (independent) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/biostatistics/kxad010) · `10 cites, 83 across 4 papers`</sub>
- **[STARsolo](https://github.com/alexdobin/STAR/blob/master/docs/STARsolo.md)** `benchmarked-sota`: Single-cell mode built into the STAR aligner that does barcode and UMI correction, gene, intron-inclusive (GeneFull) and velocity counting, optional multi-gene read allocation and Cell Ranger-like cell filtering in one pass
  <sub>Recovers: reads from genes with multi-mapping loci (EM or uniform allocation) that unique-only counting drops; intronic counts for nuclei · Gain: Kuijpers 2024 (8 SPLiT-seq pipelines): nearly identical to Parse splitpipe and one of two recommended; Du 2020: STAR detected more genes and agreed better with RNA-FISH than kallisto on Drop-seq, Fluidigm and 10x data; preprint: replicates Cell Ranger and is considerably faster (self-reported) (independent) · [code](https://github.com/alexdobin/STAR) · [preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.05.05.442755) · `432 cites, 554 across 4 papers | 2,273 stars`</sub>
- **[kallisto | bustools (kb-python)](https://www.kallistobus.tools)** `benchmarked`: Pseudoalignment of reads to a transcriptome (or nascent plus mature, 'nac', index) written to BUS files, with barcode correction, UMI collapsing and counting in bustools, wrapped by kb-python
  <sub>Recovers: speed and constant memory; higher gene detection per cell than Cell Ranger in many non-model organisms (Shainer 2021) · Gain: Shainer 2021 (22 public 10x datasets, 8 organisms): higher alignment and gene detection rates than Cell Ranger; in zebrafish pineal data it resolved an extra photoreceptor type; Bruning 2022: 4-6x faster than Cell Ranger 6; Du 2020: ~4x faster and 7.7x less memory than STAR (independent) · [code](https://github.com/pachterlab/kb_python) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-021-00870-2) · `511 cites, 520 across 2 papers | 193 stars`</sub>
- **[zUMIs](https://github.com/sdparekh/zUMIs)** `benchmarked`: STAR-based pipeline for UMI protocols (plate and droplet) that counts exonic and intronic reads separately and together, with optional downsampling to equal depth
  <sub>Recovers: more genes and counts per cell from intronic reads · Gain: You 2021: recovered the most genes and counts across datasets and, with salmon alevin and scPipe, slightly higher silhouette widths on cell-line mixtures (independent) · [code](https://github.com/sdparekh/zUMIs) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giy059) · `380 cites | 298 stars`</sub>
- **[alevin-fry / simpleaf (salmon alevin successor)](https://alevin-fry.readthedocs.io)** `benchmarked`: Lightweight mapping (pseudoalignment with structural constraints or selective alignment) to a spliced plus intronic (splici) reference, with EM resolution of gene-ambiguous UMIs and spliced, unspliced and ambiguous counts in one run; simpleaf wraps it
  <sub>Recovers: gene-ambiguous UMIs allocated by EM rather than discarded; nascent counts for nuclei and velocity from the same run · Gain: You 2021: among the top 3 workflows (with Cell Ranger) on one downstream ranking, > 50x faster than dropSeqPipe at 600M reads; self-reported (simulated 10x): mean relative false-positive expression per cell 0.011 with splici vs 0.328 for kallisto|bustools on a spliced-only index; ~2.6x faster and ~6.3x less memory than STARsolo (independent) · [code](https://github.com/COMBINE-lab/alevin-fry) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-022-01408-3) · `111 cites, 396 across 3 papers | 219 stars`</sub>
- **[Optimus (HCA / WARP pipeline)](https://broadinstitute.github.io/warp/docs/Pipelines/Optimus_Pipeline/README)** `benchmarked`: Human Cell Atlas WDL pipeline for 10x data, STARsolo-based in current versions (multi-mappers allocated Uniform by default), that returns count matrices with emptyDrops results (lower bound 100 UMIs) and QC metrics attached rather than a pre-filtered matrix

## Tiers

| Tier | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `standard` | default practice in widely used pipelines and best-practice guides |
| `benchmarked-sota` | outperformed alternatives in at least one independent benchmark |
| `benchmarked` | evaluated in at least one independent benchmark without being among the best (or with mixed results) |
| `published-unbenchmarked` | peer-reviewed or preprinted, not yet independently benchmarked |
| `hypothesis` | proposed, plausible, untested or anecdotal; doi/pmid optional |

A gain is labelled with who measured it: `independent` measured by a benchmark or study by other authors; `self-reported` measured by the method's own authors; `none` no quantitative gain reported.

## How this list is built

Every entry is a hand-written YAML record; everything else is generated from the records and is never edited by hand.

```
curation/records/*.yaml   the records, one file per research track
curation/schema.yaml      the vocabulary every record is validated against
curation/benchmarks.yaml  benchmark studies the records point at
        |
        v
build.py             validate, merge cached metadata -> data/catalog.json, .tsv
verify_citations.py  fetch each DOI (Crossref) and PMID (PubMed), match the
                     title, check both name one work -> docs/citation-check.md
enrich.py            citation counts (OpenAlex), repository activity (GitHub)
render.py            README.md, docs/index.html (the site), chapter tables,
                     dist/hub.html
```

- **Validate.** `make build` rejects a record with a missing required field, a value outside the schema's vocabulary, a malformed id or identifier, a benchmark reference that does not resolve, or an id already used in another file. Nothing is written until every record passes.
- **Verify.** A record says `verified: true` only when its DOI or PMID was fetched and the returned title matched `cite_title`. `make verify` re-checks that against Crossref and PubMed (cached; only a 404 counts as broken), and `make build-strict` refuses to ship any unverified record other than a `hypothesis` or a tool, method or design that cites no primary paper and links a homepage, repository or supporting papers instead, and any record the last check contradicted.
- **Render.** `make curate` rebuilds the catalog and every generated page offline from the records and the caches; `make all` refreshes the caches first.

Citation counts are OpenAlex's count for the record's own primary paper, a rough popularity signal and not a quality measure. A paper claimed as primary by three or more records is a suite paper, and no count is shown.

`make scview-import` and `make scview-proposal` are maintainer tooling: they compare the catalog with a local checkout of [scView](https://github.com/thirtysix/scView) and need its research files and a hand-written map that are not in either repository. Nothing else depends on them.
