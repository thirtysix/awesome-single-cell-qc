## Single-cell QC and preprocessing

### Hashing demultiplexing

- <a id="htodemux"></a>**[HTODemux (Seurat)](https://satijalab.org/seurat/articles/hashing_vignette)** `standard`: Seurat function that CLR-normalises hashtag counts, clusters droplets with k-medoids, fits a negative binomial to each tag's lowest cluster and calls the tag positive above that background's 0.99 quantile
  <sub>Recovers: sample labels for hashed cells; flags cross-sample doublets and tagless negatives · Gain: Lowering positive.quantile from 0.99 to 0.9 raised overall classification accuracy from 0.54 to 0.65 on antibody-hashed nuclei of four cell lines (Mylka 2022); mean F 0.892, 0.794 and 0.742 on three BAL batches against vireo genotype truth, with under 20% of genetic doublets called singlets (Howitt 2023) (independent) · [code](https://github.com/satijalab/seurat) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-018-1603-1) · `1,045 cites | 2,807 stars`</sub>
- <a id="hashsolo"></a>**[HashSolo](https://scanpy.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/scanpy.external.pp.hashsolo.html)** `standard`: Bayesian hashing demultiplexer in scanpy (sc.external.pp.hashsolo) that models signal and noise tag counts and scores each droplet's top tags under negative, singlet and doublet hypotheses with user priors
  <sub>Recovers: singlet calls with few negatives · Gain: Howitt 2023 (vireo truth): mean F 0.920, 0.783 and 0.737 on three BAL batches and fewer negatives than any caller except BFFcluster, but about 30% (BAL) and 60% (cell lines) of genetic doublets were called singlets (independent) · [code](https://github.com/calico/solo) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2020.05.010) · `280 cites | 98 stars | archived`</sub>
- <a id="hasheddrops"></a>**[hashedDrops (DropletUtils)](https://bioconductor.org/packages/DropletUtils)** `standard`: DropletUtils function that removes ambient tag levels and calls singlets and doublets from the log-fold change between a droplet's top and second tag, with MAD-based outlier thresholds
  <sub>Recovers: more singlets when its confidence threshold is lowered · Gain: With confident.min lowered from 2 to 0.5 it had the highest mean F in 4 of 6 batches in Howitt 2023 (BAL batch 2: 0.847 versus 0.613 at the default), at the cost of roughly doubling genetic doublets called singlets (about 3 000 extra in BAL batch 3) (independent) · [code](https://github.com/MarioniLab/DropletUtils) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.18129/B9.bioc.DropletUtils) · `68 stars`</sub>
- <a id="demuxem"></a>**[demuxEM](https://github.com/lilab-bcb/demuxEM)** `benchmarked-sota`: Hashing demultiplexer built for nucleus hashing that estimates each tag's background from empty droplets and uses EM to split a droplet's tag counts into background and true signal before calling
  <sub>Recovers: singlets from noisy nucleus hashing · Gain: Self-reported on hashed brain nuclei: 96% agreement with demuxlet versus 92% for Seurat, 94.8% agreement with Xist-based sex. In hadge 2024 it matched vireo donors best together with MULTI-seq on hashed brain nuclei (one dataset) (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/lilab-bcb/demuxEM) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-10756-2) · `189 cites | 2 stars`</sub>
- <a id="gmm-demux"></a>**[GMM-Demux](https://github.com/CHPGenetics/GMM-demux)** `benchmarked-sota`: Command-line tool that fits a two-component Gaussian mixture to each CLR-normalised tag and models droplet formation to estimate multiplet rates and flag multiplet-induced (phony) clusters
  <sub>Recovers: singlet calls with confidence scores; an estimate of undetectable same-sample multiplets for planning · Gain: Howitt 2023: mean F 0.905, 0.770, 0.720 (BAL) and 0.823 (cell lines), under 20% of genetic doublets called singlets, judged close to demuxmix and HTODemux. Self-reported: PBMC multiplet rate cut from 23.9% to 6.45%, then 3.29% after removing 9 phony clusters (independent) · [code](https://github.com/CHPGenetics/GMM-demux) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-020-02084-2) · `74 cites | 19 stars`</sub>
- <a id="demultiplex2"></a>**[deMULTIplex2](https://github.com/Gartner-Lab/deMULTIplex2)** `benchmarked-sota`: R package that models tag cross-contamination from its physical mechanism with two negative binomial GLMs (tag-count and cosine-similarity spaces) fitted by EM, giving each cell a posterior sample probability
  <sub>Recovers: singlets from noisy, imbalanced or low-cell samples, including samples whose tag counts are not bimodal · Gain: Self-reported: 63.2% versus 50.8% of all cells correctly retrieved (deMULTIplex) on multiplexed breast-cancer PDX, including two samples other callers missed; over 99% recall versus 52% (HTODemux) and 73% (demuxmix naive) in a 30-sample imbalanced simulation; accuracy held with about 10 cells per sample and with 5% of tag reads (median 30 tags per cell) on clean data (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/Gartner-Lab/deMULTIplex2) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-024-03177-y) · `34 cites, 68 across 3 papers | 21 stars`</sub>
- <a id="demuxmix"></a>**[demuxmix](https://bioconductor.org/packages/demuxmix)** `benchmarked-sota`: Bioconductor package that fits negative binomial regression mixture models to raw tag counts, optionally with each droplet's detected-gene count as a covariate, and returns a posterior error probability for every assignment
  <sub>Recovers: weakly tagged singlets with normal RNA; supports pooling one untagged sample with tagged ones · Gain: Best overall of 7 callers in Howitt 2023 (mean F 0.907, 0.833, 0.775 on BAL; under 20% of genetic doublets called singlets). Self-reported: 94% of singlets classified versus 87-93% for other callers (brain nuclei) and 85% versus 67-81% (cell-line mixture); untagged CSF cells pooled with tagged PBMC separated at sensitivity 0.980 and specificity 0.985 (independent) · [code](https://github.com/huklein/demuxmix) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btad481) · `30 cites, 61 across 2 papers | 5 stars`</sub>
- <a id="cellhashr"></a>**[cellhashR (BFF_raw, BFF_cluster, consensus)](https://github.com/BimberLab/cellhashR)** `benchmarked`: R package that runs several hashing callers, including its own bimodal flexible fitting (BFF_raw, BFF_cluster), with tag QC plots and a consensus call across callers
  <sub>Recovers: singlets agreed by several callers, a hedge when no single caller is trusted · Gain: Self-reported: BFF accurate and consistent on two reference datasets; no independent test of the consensus mode found (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/BimberLab/cellhashR) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btac213) · `38 cites | 32 stars`</sub>
- <a id="multiseqdemux"></a>**[MULTIseqDemux / deMULTIplex](https://github.com/chris-mcginnis-ucsf/MULTI-seq)** `benchmarked`: Classifier released with MULTI-seq (deMULTIplex R package, ported to Seurat as MULTIseqDemux) that sets each tag's threshold between kernel-density peaks by a quantile sweep that maximises singlets, iterating after removing negatives
  <sub>Recovers: sample labels for lipid- or antibody-tagged cells and nuclei · Gain: Preferred caller in Mylka 2022 (freemuxlet truth): overall classification accuracy 0.96 on antibody-hashed cell lines and 0.84 versus 0.74 for HTODemux on cholesterol-oligo-hashed nuclei; with demuxEM, the closest match to vireo donors on hashed brain nuclei in hadge 2024 (independent) · [code](https://github.com/chris-mcginnis-ucsf/MULTI-seq) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0433-8) · `66 stars`</sub>
- <a id="htoreader-hybrid"></a>**[HTOreader and hybrid hashing-genotype demultiplexing](https://github.com/WilsonImmunologyLab/HTOreader)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Hashing caller whose cut-offs resist many tags and unequal sample sizes, plus a hybrid strategy that names souporcell genotype clusters by their dominant hashtag and labels every cell that either method calls a singlet
  <sub>Recovers: hashing negatives and false hashing doublets in genetically distinct pools; one unhashed sample, or unstained aliquots of hashed donors · Gain: Self-reported, 8-donor B and T cell pool: the hybrid added 5.2% singlets, mostly cells hashing had called doublets (715) or negatives (166); 88.03% of cells (13 777 of 15 650) assigned across a hashed and an unhashed pool sharing donors; about 100 hashtagged cells per donor sufficed to name clusters (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/WilsonImmunologyLab/HTOreader) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbae254) · `11 cites, 22 across 2 papers | 3 stars`</sub>
- <a id="hadge"></a>**[hadge (nf-core/hadge)](https://nf-co.re/hadge)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Nextflow pipeline running 7 hashing and 5 genotype demultiplexers, scoring their agreement, with a rescue mode that labels genotype clusters by their matched hashtags
  <sub>Recovers: hashing negatives and whole weakly tagged samples when donors differ genetically · Gain: Self-reported on hashed multiple sclerosis brain nuclei: rescue mode called 90% of droplets singlets and rescued 89.7% of hashing negatives, doubling singlets for the weakest hashtags; after the original study's QC, 3 208 cells were kept, 952 of them previously excluded (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/nf-core/hadge) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-024-03249-z) · `8 cites | 26 stars`</sub>
- <a id="demuxsnp"></a>**[demuxSNP](https://bioconductor.org/packages/demuxSNP)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Bioconductor package that trains a nearest-neighbour classifier (Jaccard distance on SNP profiles) on confident hashing singlets and simulated doublets, then reassigns negative, uncertain and doublet cells
  <sub>Recovers: hashing negatives and uncertain cells with good RNA when samples differ genetically · Gain: Self-reported: beat hashing-only callers on a low-quality hashing benchmark and recovered more high-RNA-quality cells; in a renal cancer dataset HTODemux had left 2 582 cells (over 10%) negative; more robust than genotype-free and hybrid methods under class imbalance at 5-50% doublets (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/michaelplynch/demuxSNP) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giae090) · `1 cites | 9 stars`</sub>
- <a id="cmddemux"></a>**[CMDdemux](https://github.com/jiananwehi/CMDdemux)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Demultiplexer that CLR-normalises tags within each cell, clusters with K-medoids and classifies by Mahalanobis distance using hashtag and mRNA data, with plots for checking doubtful droplets
  <sub>Recovers: singlets on low-quality or over-labelled hashing data · Gain: Self-reported: 81% concordance with vireo versus 79% for deMULTIplex2 on poorly hashed ovarian tumours, both ahead of other callers; on an over-labelled CellPlex PDX dataset recovered CMO301 singlets that HTODemux and demuxmix under-called while deMULTIplex2 and BFF failed (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/jiananwehi/CMDdemux) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkag360)</sub>
- <a id="tag-guided-cell-rescue"></a>**[Tag-guided rescue of low-RNA cells](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0433-8)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Using a confident sample tag as independent evidence that a barcode holds a cell, to keep low-RNA barcodes that RNA-only cell calling or UMI floors would discard
  <sub>Recovers: cells with little RNA (neutrophils, small or stressed cells) below the usual UMI cut-off · Gain: Self-reported in MULTI-seq: in cryopreserved PDX samples, 90.8% of sequenced neutrophils were below the CellRanger UMI inflection (1 350) and were kept because their barcodes classified; low-RNA barcodes that did not classify looked like broken cells (self-reported) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0433-8)</sub>

### Genotype demultiplexing

- <a id="demuxlet"></a>**[demuxlet / freemuxlet (popscle)](https://github.com/statgen/popscle)** `standard`: Reference-genotype demultiplexer (popscle suite, with freemuxlet as its genotype-free mode) that scores each droplet's SNP reads against every donor and donor pair
  <sub>Recovers: donor labels and between-donor doublets from SNP reads · Gain: Self-reported: in simulation 50 SNPs per cell assigned 97% of singlets and detected 92% of doublets in pools of up to 64; with genotypes for 8 pooled samples over 99% of singlets were assigned correctly (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/statgen/popscle) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.4042) · `1,143 cites | 59 stars`</sub>
- <a id="souporcell"></a>**[souporcell](https://github.com/wheaton5/souporcell)** `benchmarked-sota`: Genotype-free demultiplexer that calls variants from the pooled reads, clusters cells by allele counts with an explicit ambient-RNA term, and flags cross-genotype doublets
  <sub>Recovers: donor or cell-line labels for every cell with enough variant reads, independent of tags; an ambient RNA estimate · Gain: Independent (Demuxafy): with vireo, unaffected by simulated ambient RNA in pools of up to 16 donors, but performance drops above 32 donors. Self-reported: 6 612 of 6 622 singlets and 415 of 451 doublets correct in a synthetic mixture; a minority sample resolved down to 0.6% of cells (independent) · [code](https://github.com/wheaton5/souporcell) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-0820-1) · `502 cites, 505 across 2 papers | 215 stars`</sub>
- <a id="vireo"></a>**[vireo (with cellSNP-lite)](https://vireosnp.readthedocs.io)** `benchmarked-sota`: Bayesian variational model that assigns cells to donors from allele counts (cellSNP-lite pileups) with full, partial or no reference genotypes and calls between-donor doublets
  <sub>Recovers: donor or cell-line labels independent of tags; doublets between genotypes · Gain: Most accurate and fastest of vireo, souporcell, freemuxlet and scSplit on simulated snRNA-seq pools (Fu 2025); best single method without reference genotypes at 16 or more donors (Demuxafy); on pooled tumours, 99.0-99.9% singlet recall with bulk RNA-seq genotypes and 99.2% of its doublet calls true versus 31.9% for demuxlet (Weber 2021) (independent) · [code](https://github.com/single-cell-genetics/vireo) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-019-1865-2) · `344 cites | 105 stars`</sub>
- <a id="demuxalot"></a>**[Demuxalot](https://github.com/arogozhnikov/demuxalot)** `benchmarked-sota`: Reference-genotype demultiplexer that refines donor genotypes from the pooled reads before assigning cells, built for large pools
  <sub>Recovers: donor labels with the lowest error among genotype tools in Demuxafy · Gain: Independent (Demuxafy): Demuxalot (refined) had the fewest wrong droplets, about 1% at 2 donors and 3% at 16 or more, and the best detection of between-donor doublets; combined with Dropulation it raised MCC by about 0.09 over single methods (independent) · [code](https://github.com/arogozhnikov/demuxalot) · [preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.05.22.443646) · `18 cites | 27 stars`</sub>
- <a id="demuxafy"></a>**[Demuxafy](https://demultiplexing-doublet-detecting-docs.readthedocs.io)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Containerised framework that runs 7 genotype demultiplexers and 7 transcriptional doublet detectors, combines them by intersection, and recommends combinations by pool design
  <sub>Recovers: more correctly classified singlets and doublets than any single method · Gain: Self-reported on 74 PBMC pools (12-16 donors, ~20 000 droplets), fibroblast pools and 70 in silico pools of 2-128 donors: recommended combinations raised MCC by about 0.09 over single methods (FDR < 0.05); demultiplexing alone leaves 173 (16 donors) to 1 325 (2 donors) same-donor, different-cell-type doublets per 20 000-droplet capture (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/drneavin/Demultiplexing_Doublet_Detecting_Docs) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-024-03224-8) · `87 cites | 20 stars`</sub>

### Combinatorial indexing & split-pool

- <a id="split-seq-parse"></a>**[SPLiT-seq / Parse Evercode](https://www.parsebiosciences.com)** `benchmarked`: Fixed cells or nuclei are barcoded in situ through rounds of split-pool ligation in plates, so the first-round well doubles as a sample barcode without hashing
  <sub>Recovers: samples per run (96-384 per commercial kit, vendor-reported) and storage of fixed cells; fewer stressed cells than unfixed droplet capture in one organoid comparison · Gain: Independent: about 2x genes per cell against 10x 3' on mouse thymocytes (Filippov 2024); post-QC retention 95.6% against 87.2% and fewer stressed cells than 10x CellPlex on cerebellar organoids (Sarieva 2026); self-reported: 0.1% mouse-human collisions and about $0.01 per cell library preparation (2018) (independent) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aam8999) · `1,422 cites, 1,473 across 4 papers`</sub>
- <a id="sci-rna-seq3"></a>**[sci-RNA-seq3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-0969-x)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Three-level single-cell combinatorial indexing of fixed nuclei (reverse-transcription, ligation and PCR barcodes) that scales exponentially with plate size
  <sub>Recovers: cells per experiment at a reagent cost near one cent per cell; reverse-transcription wells act as sample barcodes · Gain: About 2 million nuclei from 61 mouse embryos in one experiment (2019); optimised protocol (Martin 2023): reagent cost about 1 cent per cell or less, DSP/methanol fixation gives more UMIs than paraformaldehyde, and new buffers reduce nuclei loss during washes (self-reported) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-0969-x) · `4,465 cites, 4,598 across 2 papers`</sub>
- <a id="scifi-rna-seq"></a>**[scifi-RNA-seq](https://github.com/epigen/scifiRNA-seq)** `published-unbenchmarked`: One round of in-cell preindexing followed by deliberately overloaded droplets, so several cells per droplet are resolved by the pair of preindex and droplet barcodes
  <sub>Recovers: cells per channel: transcriptomes from multi-cell droplets are kept instead of discarded as doublets · Gain: Up to 150,000 cells per Chromium channel and over 1 million per chip; 100-fold overloading kept a stable emulsion; recovered transcriptomes scaled linearly from 15,300 to 765,000 loaded nuclei; no loss of complexity in droplets holding up to 15 nuclei (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/epigen/scifiRNA-seq) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-021-01153-z) · `270 cites | 13 stars`</sub>
- <a id="uda-seq"></a>**[UDA-seq](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02586-y)** `published-unbenchmarked`: Overloaded droplets are barcoded first and a plate-based post-indexing round then splits each droplet barcode into single cells, across RNA, ATAC and multiome modalities
  <sub>Recovers: cells and samples per channel at a low collision rate · Gain: Collision rate 1.23% with 96-well post-indexing against 6.29% expected from the droplet barcode alone; over 100,000 cells from three dozen frozen clinical biopsies in one channel (self-reported) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02586-y) · `54 cites`</sub>

### Alignment & quantification

- <a id="intronic-read-counting"></a>**[Counting intronic (nascent) reads, especially for nuclei](https://doi.org/10.1093/biostatistics/kxad010)** `standard`: Counting reads that fall in introns towards the gene (Cell Ranger include-introns, default from v7.0; STARsolo GeneFull; alevin-fry splici/USA mode; kb nac index) instead of discarding them
  <sub>Recovers: library size and genes in single-nucleus data, where much of the RNA is unspliced; cell types that exon-only counting blurs · Gain: Kuo 2024 (independent, 10x mouse cortex nuclei): exon-only references gave significantly smaller library sizes and discordant SingleR labels for astrocytes vs neural stem cells; Pool 2023: intronic read handling is one of three causes of missing genes in droplet data (independent) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/biostatistics/kxad010) · `10 cites, 83 across 4 papers`</sub>

  <sub>Recovers: gene-ambiguous UMIs allocated by EM rather than discarded; nascent counts for nuclei and velocity from the same run · Gain: You 2021: among the top 3 workflows (with Cell Ranger) on one downstream ranking, > 50x faster than dropSeqPipe at 600M reads; self-reported (simulated 10x): mean relative false-positive expression per cell 0.011 with splici vs 0.328 for kallisto|bustools on a spliced-only index; ~2.6x faster and ~6.3x less memory than STARsolo (independent) · [code](https://github.com/COMBINE-lab/alevin-fry) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-022-01408-3) · `111 cites, 396 across 3 papers | 219 stars`</sub>

### Normalisation

- <a id="scran-pooling"></a>**[scran pooled size factors (deconvolution)](https://bioconductor.org/packages/scran)** `standard`: Sums counts over pools of similar cells to estimate pool size factors, then deconvolves them into per-cell size factors that are robust to many zero counts
  <sub>Gain: More accurate cell-specific size factors than library size or bulk methods on simulations (self-reported); pipeComp rated most normalisations, scran included, as fair (self-reported) · [code](https://github.com/MarioniLab/scran) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-016-0947-7) · `1,106 cites | 48 stars`</sub>
- <a id="concept-normalisation"></a>**[Normalisation and variance-stabilising transformation](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.4292)** `standard`: Adjusting each cell's counts for its sampling efficiency (size factors) and transforming them so that variance is comparable across the expression range, usually by a shifted logarithm, Pearson residuals or a count model
  <sub>[paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.4292) · `436 cites, 4,324 across 4 papers`</sub>
- <a id="ahlmann-eltze-2023"></a>**[Ahlmann-Eltze and Huber 2023, comparison of transformations](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01814-1)** `standard`: Benchmark of 22 transformations from four families (delta method, model residuals, latent expression, factor analysis) on simulated and real data, scored by recovery of each cell's nearest neighbours
  <sub>Gain: The shifted logarithm log(y/s + 1) followed by PCA performed as well as or better than the more sophisticated alternatives; Pearson residuals performed similarly well; shifted log on CPM performed poorly (independent) · [code](https://github.com/const-ae/transformGamPoi) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01814-1) · `147 cites | 22 stars`</sub>
- <a id="sctransform"></a>**[sctransform](https://satijalab.org/seurat/articles/sctransform_vignette)** `benchmarked-sota`: Regularised negative binomial regression of each gene on sequencing depth whose Pearson residuals replace log-normalised values and rank variable genes
  <sub>Gain: pipeComp: best separability of subpopulations and the strongest removal of library-size and detection-rate effects among normalisations tested; Ahlmann-Eltze 2023: Pearson residuals as good as, not better than, the shifted log (independent) · [code](https://github.com/satijalab/sctransform) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-019-1874-1) · `4,462 cites, 5,094 across 2 papers | 272 stars`</sub>

  <sub>Gain: Ahlmann-Eltze 2023: performed as well as the shifted log and better than latent-expression and factor-analysis transformations, and as well as exact residuals at lower cost; self-reported: strongly outperformed other methods at finding biologically variable genes (independent) · [code](https://github.com/scverse/scanpy) · [paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-021-02451-7) · `194 cites | 2,594 stars`</sub>
