# Recovering cells at demultiplexing

Demultiplexing is the first place a multiplexed experiment loses cells, and the losses are larger and less random than they look. On well-hashed pools the main callers agree and lose little: in the cleanest batch of an independent benchmark every hashtag caller scored a mean F of 0.88 to 0.94 against genotype truth (Howitt 2023, [howitt-2023-hto-benchmark](../records.md#howitt-2023-hto-benchmark)), and antibody-hashed cell lines left only 1.1 to 1.8% of cells as tagless negatives (Mylka 2022, [mylka-2022-hashing-comparison](../records.md#mylka-2022-hashing-comparison)). On weak hashing the picture changes. Lipid tags left 7 to 21% of cells negative on the same cell lines, antibody tags left 33% of nuclei negative, and on a poorly hashed tumour batch the callers' mean F ranged from 0 to 0.68 while genotypes still placed 90% of droplets as singlets (Howitt 2023). Three levers recover most of what is lost. First, the caller and its normalisation: per-cell instead of per-tag normalisation recovered about 56 to 97% of the cells wrongly called negative for the tag with the most variable depth in one benchmark (Sayed 2025, [sayed-2025-hashtag-benchmark](../records.md#sayed-2025-hashtag-benchmark)). Second, genotypes, when samples differ genetically: a hashing plus genotype rescue recovered 89.7% of hashing negatives in hashed brain nuclei (Curion 2024, [hadge](../records.md#hadge)). Third, not discarding single cells that hashing mislabels as doublets, a problem that grows with the number of tags (Li 2024, [HTOreader and hybrid hashing-genotype demultiplexing](../records.md#htoreader-hybrid)). For a drug screen in which every condition is applied to the same cell line, only the first and third levers apply, because genotype can tell cell lines apart but not drugs.

## Standard practice

The default workflow runs one caller with default settings and keeps its singlets. In Seurat that is [HTODemux](../records.md#htodemux): tag counts are centred-log-ratio (CLR) normalised per tag, droplets are clustered, a negative binomial is fitted to each tag's background cluster, and a droplet is positive for a tag above the 0.99 quantile of that background (Stoeckius 2018). The Seurat vignette runs exactly these defaults; on its 8-tag example they give 13 972 singlets, 2 598 doublets and 346 negatives. [MULTIseqDemux / deMULTIplex](../records.md#multiseqdemux) (the MULTI-seq classifier, McGinnis 2019) is the other Seurat option. In Python, scanpy ships [HashSolo](../records.md#hashsolo); in Bioconductor, DropletUtils provides [hashedDrops](../records.md#hasheddrops).

When samples come from different people or different cell lines, genotype demultiplexing is the standard alternative or complement. [demuxlet / freemuxlet](../records.md#demuxlet) needs reference genotypes; in its original simulation 50 SNPs per cell assigned 97% of singlets in pools of up to 64 people (Kang 2018). [vireo](../records.md#vireo) and [souporcell](../records.md#souporcell) need no reference and infer genotypes from the pooled reads (Huang 2019; Heaton 2020). For pooled cancer cell lines, the MIX-Seq design classifies cells with as few as 50 to 100 detected SNP sites and agreed with expression-based calls for over 99% of cells (McFarland 2020, see [MIX-Seq](../records.md#mix-seq)).

Standard practice discards negatives and all hashing doublets. Two facts make that costly. Hashing quality and RNA quality were "largely uncorrelated" across the Howitt datasets, so a weak tag does not mean a bad cell. And the cells that fail to tag are not a random sample: antibody-hashed mouse brain left mostly non-microglial cells negative, dying high-mitochondrial PBMC clusters hashed worse (Mylka 2022), and two neuronal clusters among hashed brain nuclei consisted mostly or entirely of cells that hashing had dropped (Curion 2024).

## Benchmarked state of the art

**Hashing callers.** Howitt 2023 compared seven callers against vireo genotype assignments on six tagging experiments with antibody and lipid tags. Genotypes called 81 to 91% of droplets singlets, more than any hashtag caller except BFFcluster, which over-called singlets. [demuxmix](../records.md#demuxmix) was best overall, scoring mean F 0.907, 0.833 and 0.775 on three bronchoalveolar lavage batches and calling under 20% of genetic doublets singlets; [HTODemux](../records.md#htodemux) and [GMM-Demux](../records.md#gmm-demux) were close. Callers that assume two clean peaks per tag collapsed on weak data: BFFcluster ([cellhashR](../records.md#cellhashr)) fell to 0.119 and 0.188 on the weaker lavage batches and deMULTIplex to 0.218 on a tumour batch. [HashSolo](../records.md#hashsolo) left the fewest negatives of any caller except BFFcluster but called about 30% (lavage) to 60% (cell lines) of genetic doublets singlets. Lowering [hashedDrops](../records.md#hasheddrops)'s threshold gave the best F in four of six batches, at the price of roughly doubling doublet leakage, about 3 000 extra doublets in one batch. The authors' advice for weak hashing is to try several callers to maximise singlets, while watching doublets.

Mylka 2022 compared tag chemistries and preferred [MULTIseqDemux / deMULTIplex](../records.md#multiseqdemux): overall classification accuracy 0.96 for antibody-hashed cell lines, against 0.68 to 0.84 for lipid tags; for nuclei, cholesterol-oligo tags reached 0.84 (0.74 with HTODemux) and antibody tags 0.51 to 0.54. Changing HTODemux's positive quantile from 0.99 to 0.9 raised the antibody-nuclei accuracy from 0.54 to 0.65, a direct measure of how much the default cut-off leaves behind. Antibodies were better for spleen and lung, lipids for brain (0.85 to 0.87 against 0.38). On hashed brain nuclei, MULTI-seq and [demuxEM](../records.md#demuxem), which models each tag's background from empty droplets, matched vireo's donors best of the hashing tools, while HashSolo called almost every droplet a singlet and hashedDrops called 32 singlets out of 4 048 droplets (Curion 2024). Caller choice alone can swing yield from almost nothing to almost everything on nuclei.

Sayed 2025 crossed normalisations with callers on 12 real datasets. Normalising tags within each cell, so that a cell's shallow tag library does not push it under every threshold, separated tagged from untagged cells better than the per-tag CLR in the Seurat vignette. On the dataset with the most variable tag depth it recovered about 97% (clustering) and 56% (MULTI-seq workflow) of the cells wrongly called negative for one tag. A per-cell CLR plus clustering workflow (hashDemux) had the highest average F; its doublet calls were weak, so RNA-based doublet filtering should follow.

[deMULTIplex2](../records.md#demultiplex2) models how free tags contaminate droplets. Its own benchmark reports 63.2% of all cells correctly assigned on a multiplexed breast-cancer xenograft dataset against 50.8% for its predecessor, two samples recovered that other callers missed, over 99% recall in a 30-sample imbalanced simulation where HTODemux reached 52% and demuxmix (without RNA) 73%, and stable accuracy at about 10 cells per sample (Zhu 2024). In outside comparisons it was near the top: F 0.87 against 0.9 for the best workflow in Sayed 2025, and 79% concordance with vireo on the poorly hashed tumours against 81% for CMDdemux, both ahead of other callers (Wang 2026, [CMDdemux](../records.md#cmddemux)). The same CMDdemux comparison found deMULTIplex2 and BFF failing on an over-labelled CellPlex xenograft dataset. No single caller wins everywhere.

**Genotype callers.** On simulated single-nucleus pools, vireo was the most accurate and fastest of vireo, souporcell, freemuxlet and scSplit; all but scSplit reached 80 to 85% accuracy (Fu 2025, [fu-2025-snrna-genetic-benchmark](../records.md#fu-2025-snrna-genetic-benchmark)). In pooled tumours with heavy copy-number change, vireo with bulk RNA-seq genotypes recalled 99.0 to 99.9% of singlets, and 99.2% of its doublet calls were real against 31.9% for demuxlet (Weber 2021, [weber-2021-cancer-genetic-demux](../records.md#weber-2021-cancer-genetic-demux)). [Demuxafy](../records.md#demuxafy) compared seven genotype tools on 74 PBMC pools and 70 simulated pools of 2 to 128 donors: [Demuxalot](../records.md#demuxalot) had the fewest errors (about 1% at 2 donors, 3% at 16 or more), vireo was the best single tool without reference genotypes at 16 or more donors, souporcell and vireo were unaffected by added ambient RNA up to 16 donors, and souporcell degraded above 32 (Neavin 2024). Combining tools raised Matthews correlation by about 0.09. Precision falls as doublets rise for every genotype tool (Fu 2025): vireo's went from 100% to 77.4% between 0% and 30% simulated doublets (Weber 2021).

## Published but not independently benchmarked

**Hybrid rescue.** When samples are genetically distinct, genotype clusters can be named by the hashtag that dominates them, and every cell in a cluster then gets a sample label whatever its tag looked like. [hadge](../records.md#hadge) runs 7 hashing and 5 genotype tools in one Nextflow pipeline; its rescue mode called 90% of hashed brain nuclei singlets, rescued 89.7% of hashing negatives, doubled singlets for the weakest tags, and after the original study's QC kept 3 208 cells, 952 of which had been excluded before (Curion 2024). [HTOreader and hybrid hashing-genotype demultiplexing](../records.md#htoreader-hybrid) added 5.2% singlets to an 8-donor pool, most of them cells hashing had called doublets (715) or negatives (166), and assigned 88% of cells across a hashed pool and an unhashed pool from the same donors; about 100 hashtagged cells per donor were enough to name the clusters (Li 2024). [demuxSNP](../records.md#demuxsnp) trains a nearest-neighbour classifier on confident hashing singlets' SNP profiles and reassigns negatives, uncertain cells and doublets; on renal cancer data HTODemux had left over 10% of cells negative (Lynch 2024). All three need genetic differences between samples; souporcell, the clustering step in HTOreader, produced wrong clusters at high doublet rates in two of these papers.

**Using the tag to keep low-RNA cells.** MULTI-seq showed that a confident tag is evidence a barcode holds a cell even when its RNA is too low for standard cell calling: 90.8% of sequenced neutrophils in cryopreserved xenografts fell below the Cell Ranger UMI inflection and were kept because their barcodes classified, while unclassified low-RNA barcodes looked like broken cells (McGinnis 2019, [Tag-guided rescue of low-RNA cells](../records.md#tag-guided-cell-rescue)). The cell-calling side of this is covered under [EmptyDrops](../records.md#emptydrops) and [CellBender remove-background](../records.md#cellbender).

**Newer callers.** [CMDdemux](../records.md#cmddemux) normalises tags within each cell, clusters, classifies by Mahalanobis distance with mRNA information and reports gains on low-quality and over-labelled data (Wang 2026). [GMM-Demux](../records.md#gmm-demux) also estimates how many same-sample doublets remain undetectable, and flagged 9 multiplet-made clusters in PBMC (Xin 2020). Several 2023 to 2026 tools were noted but not given records: scDemultiplex (beta-binomial, PMID 37664174), Ensemblex (genotype ensemble for 4 to 80 lines; 90.12% of cells correctly classified, PMID 40611203), Souporcell3 (up to 64 donors, PMID 41808435), mitoSplitter (mitochondrial variants, used on five lung cancer lines under a BET degrader, PMID 37725654), fastdemux (preprint, PMID 41726916) and CellBouncer [CellBouncer](../records.md#cellbouncer).

## Hypotheses and open questions

**Doublets.** A true cross-condition doublet cannot be rescued; its RNA is a mixture of two conditions. Studies that use hashing doublets as ground truth found 73% of hashing multiplets were missed by every RNA-based detector (Ttoouli 2025, PMID 41166377).

**Assigning untagged cells from their transcriptome.** For fixed identities this works: cell-line calls from expression and from SNPs agreed for over 99% of MIX-Seq cells, and expression-aware demultiplexing of donors reached mean accuracy 0.98 when combined with genetics (Nassiri 2024, PMID 38911824). For treatment labels it is circular: a cell assigned to a drug because it looks treated will inflate that drug's effect and hide non-responders ([expression-label-recovery](../records.md#expression-label-recovery)).

**Gaps.** No benchmark has tested callers at more than about 24 tags per pool, on drug-treated cells, or on row-by-column tag designs. In a two-group design a well is known only when both the row call and the column call are singlets, so well-level yield is roughly the product of the two singlet rates; this follows from the design and has not been measured (designs are covered under [sci-Plex](../records.md#sci-plex), [MULTI-seq](../records.md#multi-seq) and [SPLiT-seq / Parse Evercode](../records.md#split-seq-parse)). The consensus mode of [cellhashR](../records.md#cellhashr) and the intersections in [Demuxafy](../records.md#demuxafy) have not been compared with single callers on hashing data. No evidence was found that the tag-counting tool (Cell Ranger, CITE-seq-Count, kallisto) changes recovery.

## Practical recommendations for low-cell multiplexed drug screens

Ranked by expected gain per unit of effort, for screens with many conditions and few cells per condition.

1. **Diagnose before choosing.** Plot each tag's count distribution and a tag-space embedding, then tabulate negatives, doublets and median tag UMIs per condition (Howitt 2023 gives the plots). Gain: none by itself, but it shows whether losses are small and even (then stop) or large and condition-specific. Needs: tag counts.
2. **Re-call with per-cell normalisation and more than one caller.** Run HTODemux on per-cell CLR (Seurat margin 2), [demuxmix](../records.md#demuxmix) with the RNA covariate, and [deMULTIplex2](../records.md#demultiplex2); keep cells that agree, inspect cells on which they disagree. Do not tune thresholds just to raise singlet counts (hashedDrops doubled doublet leakage that way). Expected gain: little on clean data (all callers within about 0.06 F of each other in Howitt 2023), tens of percent of negatives on weak or imbalanced data (Sayed 2025; Zhu 2024, self-reported). Needs: tag counts, RNA counts.
3. **Add genotypes if the pool holds more than one cell line or donor.** Run [vireo](../records.md#vireo) or [souporcell](../records.md#souporcell) (or [Demuxalot](../records.md#demuxalot) with reference genotypes) and rescue with [hadge](../records.md#hadge), [HTOreader and hybrid hashing-genotype demultiplexing](../records.md#htoreader-hybrid) or [demuxSNP](../records.md#demuxsnp). Expected gain: 70 to 90% of hashing negatives in the one dataset measured (Curion 2024) plus misfiled doublets. Genotype recovers the cell line, not the drug: a rescued cell gets a condition only if each line received one condition in that pool. Needs: BAM, genetically distinct samples.
4. **Look at low-RNA tagged barcodes before RNA QC.** Treated cells may shrink or lose RNA; keep confidently tagged low-RNA barcodes for review instead of cutting them at a UMI floor ([Tag-guided rescue of low-RNA cells](../records.md#tag-guided-cell-rescue)). Gain: cell-type dependent. Needs: raw matrix and tags.
5. **Use expression-based assignment only for identity.** Recover cell line or cell type for negatives if needed, never the drug ([expression-label-recovery](../records.md#expression-label-recovery)).
6. **Design the next run for recovery.** Antibody tags for intact cells and lipid or cholesterol tags for nuclei and brain (Mylka 2022); titrate and process quickly (Brown 2024, PMID 38220132); balance loading across conditions; sequence tags enough, since deMULTIplex2 kept near-perfect accuracy at a median of 30 tag counts per cell on clean data. Where hashing is the bottleneck, genotype-pooled cell lines ([MIX-Seq](../records.md#mix-seq)) or well-barcoded chemistries ([sci-Plex](../records.md#sci-plex), [10x Chromium Flex](../records.md#10x-flex), [SPLiT-seq / Parse Evercode](../records.md#split-seq-parse)) avoid tag loss altogether. Statistical handling of the few cells left per condition is covered under [dreamlet](../records.md#dreamlet) and [muscat](../records.md#muscat).
