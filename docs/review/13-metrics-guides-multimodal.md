# QC metrics, guides and modality-specific QC

Scope: the remaining QC-metric and threshold tools, damaged-cell detectors, best-practice guides, and QC for other modalities (CITE-seq protein tags, scATAC and multiome, single-nucleus RNA, spatial). The core argument about fixed versus adaptive thresholds, mito fraction as a damage marker, and the nuclear-fraction signal is made in the calling and QC review ([MAD-based adaptive QC thresholds](../records.md#mad-adaptive-qc), [miQC](../records.md#miqc), [ddqc](../records.md#ddqc), [DropletQC](../records.md#dropletqc)) and is not repeated here. Doublet callers, including the ATAC-specific ones, are in the doublets review; cell calling and ambient RNA tools in the quantification and calling review.

## What this chapter adds

Three findings change how QC should be read.

**Most QC tools are reports, not models.** Of the 182 tools that scRNA-tools files under quality control, few fit a model that decides which cells to drop; most compute the same metrics and draw them. The modelling tools are a short list: per-population mixtures ([SampleQC](../records.md#sampleqc), [miQC](../records.md#miqc)), per-cluster thresholds ([ddqc](../records.md#ddqc)), supervised classifiers ([cellity](../records.md#cellity)) and nuclear-fraction methods ([DropletQC](../records.md#dropletqc), [QClus](../records.md#qclus)). The reporting tools ([SCTK-QC](../records.md#sctk-qc), [scRNABatchQC](../records.md#scrnabatchqc), [scQCEA](../records.md#scqcea), [QCatch](../records.md#qcatch), [SingleCellMQC](../records.md#singlecellmqc)) are useful for one thing the modelling tools do not do: putting many samples, or many callers, side by side so that an outlier lane or a disagreement between callers is visible.

**QC choices change biological counts by multiples.** In one public snRNA-seq dataset of 33,600 intestinal nuclei, the fraction of gamma delta T cells ranged from 3.38% to 12.49% depending on calling strategy, and from 5.46% to 21.62% under strict filtering, a spread of about fourfold; within one QC stratum the call rate rose from 0.51% to 12.17% across deciles of genes per nucleus [boloori-2026](../records.md#boloori-2026). The direction of error can also be the opposite of the salvage concern: re-analysis of six reference atlases with intronic fraction found organs where up to 85% of cells were nuclei-free, low-quality barcodes left in by the atlases' own QC [montserrat-ayuso-2024](../records.md#montserrat-ayuso-2024).

**Every modality has its own version of the same three metrics.** Library size, complexity and a damage fraction reappear as fragment count, TSS enrichment and fraction in peaks for ATAC [scATAC per-cell QC metrics](../records.md#atac-qc-metrics), as total tags and isotype or background signal for protein, and as spot-level counts judged against neighbours for spatial data [SpotSweeper](../records.md#spotsweeper). The same lesson carries over: global fixed thresholds confound biology with quality, and protocol differences move the metrics themselves [de-rop-2024](../records.md#de-rop-2024).

## Standard practice

- **Guides.** Three guides set most pipelines' defaults. The 2019 tutorial recommended judging counts, genes and mito jointly, keeping thresholds permissive and revisiting QC after clustering [luecken-theis-2019](../records.md#luecken-theis-2019). Its 2023 successor extends this across modalities and is the source of the widely copied 5-MAD rule [heumos-2023](../records.md#heumos-2023). The Bioconductor book uses per-batch MAD outliers from [scuttle](../records.md#scuttle) and adds a check that discarded cells do not form a lost cell type, by comparing their average profile with the kept cells [osca](../records.md#osca). The Hemberg-lab tutorial and course is the common teaching reference [andrews-2021-tutorial](../records.md#andrews-2021-tutorial).
- **Metric computation.** `scuttle::perCellQCMetrics` and `quickPerCellQC` in R and `scanpy.pp.calculate_qc_metrics` in Python compute the same per-cell table; neither deserves a separate verdict beyond [MAD-based adaptive QC thresholds](../records.md#mad-adaptive-qc). [SCTK-QC](../records.md#sctk-qc) runs EmptyDrops, several doublet callers and DecontX or SoupX in one pass and reports them together.
- **scATAC and multiome.** Cells are filtered on unique fragments, TSS enrichment, fraction of fragments in peaks, nucleosome signal and blacklist fraction [scATAC per-cell QC metrics](../records.md#atac-qc-metrics), computed by [ArchR](../records.md#archr) (defaults TSS enrichment of at least 4 and at least 1,000 fragments), [Signac](../records.md#signac) or [SnapATAC2](../records.md#snapatac2).
- **CITE-seq.** Protein tags are normalised per cell with CLR (Seurat's default) or with [dsb](../records.md#dsb), which uses empty droplets as the background. Cells are rarely filtered on protein alone; OSCA flags cells with outlying isotype-control or ambient-tag signal [osca](../records.md#osca).
- **Spatial (sequencing-based).** Spots are filtered with single-cell style thresholds by default; spatially aware alternatives are below.

## Benchmarked state of the art

There is still no independent benchmark of RNA QC-thresholding tools beyond pipeComp, covered in the calling and QC review [pipeComp](../records.md#pipecomp). The independent comparisons found for this track are in other modalities.

- **scATAC feature engineering.** Luo et al. compared eight configurations of five methods on scATAC and multiome data, after common ArchR-based QC. SnapATAC, SnapATAC2 and simple aggregation beat LSI-based methods; SnapATAC2 and ArchR scaled best [SnapATAC2](../records.md#snapatac2), [ArchR](../records.md#archr). One result matters for QC: after dropping components correlated above 0.75 with library size, the Signac and ArchR embeddings were still correlated 0.5-0.75 with depth, against about 0.2 for SnapATAC2's cosine embedding [Signac](../records.md#signac). In a design where conditions differ in fragment yield, depth can therefore drive clustering in LSI-based pipelines.
- **scATAC protocols.** Eight scATAC methods in 47 PBMC experiments (169,000 profiles, one pipeline) differed in library complexity and tagmentation specificity, and these differences carried into annotation, genotype demultiplexing, peak calling, differential accessibility and motif enrichment [de-rop-2024](../records.md#de-rop-2024). TSS enrichment and fraction-in-peaks thresholds are therefore protocol-specific, not universal.
- **Spatial segmentation.** Eleven segmentation methods across five imaging technologies and 15 datasets showed no single winner, and segmentation choice changed cell-type specificity, transcript contamination between neighbours and ligand-receptor results [martinez-de-morentin-2026](../records.md#martinez-de-morentin-2026). Segmentation plays the role of cell calling in imaging data, and its errors look downstream like doublets and ambient RNA.

## Published but not independently benchmarked

**Multi-population and nuclei filters.**
- [SampleQC](../records.md#sampleqc) fits a mixture model to QC metrics across groups of similar samples, giving each QC cell type its own component. In the authors' simulations the good cells that miQC and MAD rules wrongly excluded came almost entirely from one cell type, while SampleQC kept rare QC types; it ran on 867k brain nuclei from 172 samples.
- [QClus](../records.md#qclus) clusters nuclei on unspliced fraction, mito fraction and marker scores and removes empty and contaminated droplets. Against seven alternatives (Cell Ranger 7, CellBender, SampleQC, DIEM, DecontX, DropletQC, EmptyNN) on 252 heart samples and 1.9 million nuclei it scored best in the most samples, in the authors' own comparison. Its defaults are cardiac-specific.

**Classifiers and coverage-based detectors.** [cellity](../records.md#cellity) trained a support vector machine on microscopy-labelled broken and empty Fluidigm C1 captures and reported over 30% higher accuracy than thresholds; it is the only QC method with imaging ground truth, but it has not been tested on droplet data. [SkewC](../records.md#skewc) flags cells with skewed gene-body coverage, mainly useful for full-length protocols. [SinQC](../records.md#sinqc) (2016) flags cells whose expression is inconsistent with their quality metrics; no code repository was found.

**Reports.** [scRNABatchQC](../records.md#scrnabatchqc) and [scQCEA](../records.md#scqcea) compare many samples in one report, the latter adding a marker-based check that each library contains recognisable cell types. [QCatch](../records.md#qcatch) gives alevin-fry users cell calling and a QC report with splicing status. [SingleCellMQC](../records.md#singlecellmqc) runs QC at sample, cell, feature and batch level across RNA, protein tags and immune receptors, with tissue-specific reference ranges.

**Damage markers.** MALAT1, a nuclear-retained RNA, tracks the spliced-to-unspliced ratio and can stand in for nuclear fraction when BAMs are unavailable [MALAT1 as a cell-quality marker](../records.md#malat1-quality-marker); the atlas re-analysis used it the same way [montserrat-ayuso-2024](../records.md#montserrat-ayuso-2024). Both are consistent with [scQCenrich](../records.md#scqcenrich), which includes MALAT1 among its features.

**Protein tags (CITE-seq).** [ADTnorm](../records.md#adtnorm) aligns negative and positive peaks of each marker across samples and studies and scores staining quality; in its authors' comparison with 14 methods (CLR, dsb, DecontPro, totalVI, Harmony and others) on 13 datasets it aligned populations best. At the bench, titrating a 52-antibody panel raised median positive signal by 57% and lowered background from 26.3% to 14.9% of UMIs [Buus et al. 2021 antibody titration for CITE-seq](../records.md#buus-2021), so part of what ambient-tag tools such as [dsb](../records.md#dsb) and [DecontPro](../records.md#decontpro) remove can be prevented before sequencing.

**scATAC.** [scATAC-pro](../records.md#scatac-pro) computes QC at each step for any protocol. Bin-based pipelines such as SnapATAC2 cannot compute fraction in peaks; [Chromap peak-free scATAC QC](../records.md#chromap-qc) (preprint) reports it at alignment without peak calling and found low-quality cells that SnapATAC2's QC missed.

**Spatial.** [SpotSweeper](../records.md#spotsweeper) judges each spot against its neighbourhood, so a biologically low-count region such as white matter is not removed wholesale, and found Visium spots that were low quality across many public datasets. [SpatialArtifacts](../records.md#spatialartifacts) (preprint) finds contiguous artefacts such as dry patches and tissue lifting. [SpotClean](../records.md#spotclean) corrects spot swapping (bleed of UMIs between spots), the spatial analogue of ambient RNA; it needs at least 25% of spots off tissue. [CyLinter](../records.md#cylinter) removes cells affected by folds, debris and antibody aggregates in high-plex protein imaging.

## Modality-specific traps

- **Single nuclei.** Nuclei are nuclear by design, so the nuclear-fraction and MALAT1 signals that mark damaged cells in whole-cell data do not mark damage in nuclei; the relevant failure there is cytoplasmic or ambient contamination, which [QClus](../records.md#qclus) and [CellBender remove-background](../records.md#cellbender) target. Mito reads in nuclei are contamination rather than a health signal. Rare-type counts in nuclei depend strongly on genes detected per nucleus [boloori-2026](../records.md#boloori-2026).
- **Multiome.** A barcode can pass on RNA and fail on ATAC or the reverse. Filtering on the intersection is the default; when one modality is the readout, filtering on the other modality's metrics alone removes cells for a failure that does not affect the readout.
- **ATAC depth.** Fragment yield differs between protocols [de-rop-2024](../records.md#de-rop-2024) and leaks into LSI embeddings [Signac](../records.md#signac), so per-condition fragment differences need checking before clustering.
- **Protein.** Over-concentrated antibodies create background that looks like expression [Buus et al. 2021 antibody titration for CITE-seq](../records.md#buus-2021); staining batch effects need per-marker alignment rather than per-cell scaling [ADTnorm](../records.md#adtnorm).
- **Spatial.** Global thresholds remove whole tissue regions with low counts; a local comparison [SpotSweeper](../records.md#spotsweeper) and artefact masks [SpatialArtifacts](../records.md#spatialartifacts) separate the two.

## Hypotheses and open questions

- Open: no independent benchmark compares [SampleQC](../records.md#sampleqc), [miQC](../records.md#miqc), [ddqc](../records.md#ddqc) and nuclear-fraction methods on data with ground truth for damage. [cellity](../records.md#cellity)'s microscopy-labelled C1 data are the only such ground truth found, and they predate droplet platforms.
- Open: the salvage literature argues for keeping more cells; the atlas re-analysis [montserrat-ayuso-2024](../records.md#montserrat-ayuso-2024) argues that atlases keep too many. Both point to nuclear fraction as the deciding metric, which suggests the disagreement is about which metric to filter on, not about how strict to be.
- Open: no method found sets ATAC or protein QC thresholds per condition. The condition-dependence argument made for RNA [condition-aware-qc-audit](../records.md#condition-aware-qc-audit) applies equally to fragment counts and TSS enrichment if a drug changes chromatin accessibility globally.
- Open: no independent benchmark of ADT normalisation was found; [ADTnorm](../records.md#adtnorm)'s 14-method comparison is the only multi-method one and is self-reported.

## Recommendations for low-cell multiplexed drug screens

Ranked by expected gain per unit of effort. These add to the calling and QC review's list rather than repeating it.

1. **Report QC across lanes and conditions before filtering.** Run a multi-sample report ([scRNABatchQC](../records.md#scrnabatchqc), [SCTK-QC](../records.md#sctk-qc) or [SingleCellMQC](../records.md#singlecellmqc) for CITE-seq designs) and look for a lane or condition that is an outlier on every metric. *Gain:* makes a failed lane or a condition-dependent shift visible; no measured number. *Data:* filtered matrix per lane.
2. **Model QC jointly across samples when cell types differ.** Use [SampleQC](../records.md#sampleqc) or per-cluster thresholds ([ddqc](../records.md#ddqc)) rather than one global MAD rule. *Gain:* fewer rare types excluded in simulation, self-reported [SampleQC](../records.md#sampleqc). *Data:* filtered matrix, several samples.
3. **Add a nuclear-fraction or MALAT1 check to whole-cell data.** Flag cells with low intronic fraction (or low MALAT1) and low complexity as nuclei-free; do not apply the rule to nuclei. *Gain:* up to 85% low-quality cells in some atlas organs [montserrat-ayuso-2024](../records.md#montserrat-ayuso-2024). *Data:* BAM or spliced/unspliced counts, or MALAT1 counts alone [MALAT1 as a cell-quality marker](../records.md#malat1-quality-marker).
4. **For CITE-seq readouts, titrate before the screen.** *Gain:* 57% higher median positive signal and 43% less background [Buus et al. 2021 antibody titration for CITE-seq](../records.md#buus-2021). Then normalise with empty-droplet background ([dsb](../records.md#dsb)) and align markers across plates or lanes ([ADTnorm](../records.md#adtnorm)). *Data:* protein tags, raw matrix.
5. **For ATAC or multiome readouts, set thresholds per protocol and check depth by condition.** *Gain:* avoids depth-driven clusters (LSI embeddings correlated 0.5-0.75 with depth [Signac](../records.md#signac)). *Data:* fragment files.
