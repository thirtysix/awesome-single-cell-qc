# Keeping cells at cell calling and QC; damaged cells

Scope: separating cells from empty droplets, ambient RNA removal as a way to keep cells, QC thresholds, damaged and dying cells, and RNA-based doublet detection where it changes what is kept. Tag-based demultiplexing and tag doublets are covered in the demultiplexing review ([demuxmix](../records.md#demuxmix), [HTODemux](../records.md#htodemux)); statistics with few cells per condition in the low-cell review ([Pseudobulk differential expression](../records.md#pseudobulk-de)).

## How many cells are thrown away that could be kept, and how to keep them safely

Two steps decide which barcodes become cells: cell calling, then QC filtering. Both discard intact cells, and the published numbers are large.

**Cell calling.** A total-UMI threshold cannot tell a small cell from an empty droplet, so knee-style calling loses low-RNA cell types. EmptyDrops kept a putative interneuron cluster in a ~900-cell brain dataset and putative monocytes in placenta that the Cell Ranger v2 knee discarded [EmptyDrops](../records.md#emptydrops). Callers built on it report more: CB2 found 24% more cells than EmptyDrops on average (range 4-81%), 88% of them joining existing populations [CB2](../records.md#cb2), and EmptyNN recovered 885 barcodes below a 200-UMI cut, including platelets with a median of 93.5 UMIs [EmptyNN](../records.md#emptynn). These gains are self-reported. The opposite error is real too: after EmptyDrops, DropletQC still found 0.4% (PBMC) to 9.5% (mouse brain) of called barcodes to be cell-free [DropletQC](../records.md#dropletqc).

Dying cells are lost before any software sees them. In cells sorted after TNF-alpha treatment, 885 dying and 1,280 dead cells were recovered against 3,000 targeted [oflanagan-2019](../records.md#oflanagan-2019). Drug-treated cells may also carry less RNA: paclitaxel or celecoxib alone generally repressed global transcription per cell when measured against spike-ins (PMID 34159316).

**QC filtering.** Fixed mitochondrial cut-offs remove the most. A 10% cut removed 41% of cells from a menstrual blood dataset where miQC removed 19%, and 88.1% of one ovarian tumour sample [miQC](../records.md#miqc). Across many datasets, a fixed rule (<200 genes or >10% mito) retained a median 69.4% of cells against up to 95.4% for cluster-level adaptive QC [ddqc](../records.md#ddqc). A 5% mito cut misclassified healthy and low-quality cells in 13 of 44 human tissues [osorio-cai-2021](../records.md#osorio-cai-2021), and high-mito malignant cells are viable, with no more dissociation stress than other malignant cells [mito-filter-resistant-cells](../records.md#mito-filter-resistant-cells).

**Keeping them safely** means replacing single-metric cut-offs with evidence that separates damage from biology: testing barcodes against the ambient profile rather than a UMI floor, computing thresholds within cell type and batch, using the nuclear (unspliced) fraction as a damage signal, and correcting ambient RNA rather than discarding cells with mixed profiles. Each is covered below with its evidence.

## Standard practice

- **Cell calling.** Cell Ranger calls cells in two steps: an order-of-magnitude UMI cut-off for high-RNA cells, then an EmptyDrops-style test for low-RNA cells (10x Genomics documentation; [Cell Ranger](../records.md#cellranger)). STARsolo's default is a Cell Ranger 2.2-style knee; its EmptyDrops_CR option copies Cell Ranger 3/4 and hard-codes Cell Ranger's candidate limits, including a 500-UMI minimum (`umiMin`) and a minimum fraction of the median cell (`umiMinFracMedian` 0.01), so lower barcodes are never tested (STAR documentation, docs/STARsolo.md; [STARsolo](../records.md#starsolo), [Knee / inflection-point cell calling](../records.md#knee-cell-calling)). DropletUtils' `emptyDrops` treats barcodes at or below 100 UMIs as ambient and tests everything above [EmptyDrops](../records.md#emptydrops).
- **QC thresholds.** Best-practice guides use thresholds relative to each sample: the scverse guide flags cells 5 median absolute deviations (MADs) from the median on log counts, log genes and top-20-gene share, and mito only when it is both 3 MADs high and above 8%, and advises being "as permissive as possible" ([heumos-2023](../records.md#heumos-2023); sc-best-practices.org). Bioconductor's OSCA uses scuttle's MAD outliers per batch [osca](../records.md#osca). See [MAD-based adaptive QC thresholds](../records.md#mad-adaptive-qc).
- **Ambient RNA.** The scverse guide runs [SoupX](../records.md#soupx); [DecontX](../records.md#decontx) and [CellBender remove-background](../records.md#cellbender) are the common alternatives.
- **Doublets.** The scverse guide uses [scDblFinder](../records.md#scdblfinder); Scrublet and DoubletFinder remain widely used.
- **Bench side.** Dead-cell and apoptotic-cell exclusion before loading is recommended but often skipped (PMID 31603610).

## Benchmarked state of the art

**Ambient RNA.** Three independent benchmarks agree that no method wins everywhere.

- Janssen et al. pooled two mouse subspecies so that cross-genotype reads measure background directly. Background made up 3-35% of UMIs per cell, mostly ambient RNA. CellBender gave the most precise per-cell estimates and the largest gain in marker detection: the proximal tubule marker Slc34a1 appeared in ~60% of non-tubule cells before correction, 54% after SoupX, 7% after CellBender and 2% after DecontX. SoupX underestimated background and, by design, cannot capture cell-to-cell variation. Clustering was fairly robust to noise [janssen-2023](../records.md#janssen-2023).
- Cargnelli et al. tested seven methods on 64 samples (135,561 cells), including control samples without ambient RNA. 81.56% of cells carried some ambient RNA (mean load 7.79% in cells, 11.80% in nuclei). scAR (0.679) and CellBender (0.604) removed the most ambient RNA, but scAR also removed 0.445 of endogenous signal; DecontX was the only method that did not reduce cell-type recovery. They recommend CellBender, DecontX in full mode, or SoupX in reduced mode, noting that CellBender and DecontX over-correct clean samples more than SoupX [cargnelli-2026](../records.md#cargnelli-2026).
- Nie et al. tested seven tools on ten metrics. The abstract names DecontX most accurate for contamination level, scAR most robust but overestimating, and CellClear best for closely related subtypes. In their mitochondrial-genotype experiment, CellBender and FastCAR came closest to the measured baseline (mean RMSLE below 0.06); scAR reassigned ~17% of cells to other types in one dataset [nie-2026](../records.md#nie-2026).
- Two 2026 preprints extend this, so the summary below is not final. Schroeder et al. added a well-plate platform and a count-integrity check: CellBender and SoupX removed contamination reliably with minimal distortion, while scAR created spurious cell types and CellClear replaced most counts [schroeder-2026](../records.md#schroeder-2026). Meier et al. found doublet detection worked best before ambient correction, except with CellBender [meier-2026](../records.md#meier-2026). Both are covered in the quantification and ambient review.

Readers should note the conflict inside Nie et al.: abstract and results name different winners for contamination accuracy. On present evidence, [CellBender remove-background](../records.md#cellbender) and [DecontX](../records.md#decontx) are benchmarked state of the art, [scAR](../records.md#scar) is the most aggressive (good removal, real over-correction risk), and [SoupX](../records.md#soupx) is the conservative standard.

**Doublets.** Xi and Li benchmarked nine detectors on 16 real datasets with annotated doublets and 112 synthetic ones; DoubletFinder was most accurate and cxds fastest [xi-li-2021](../records.md#xi-li-2021). In their protocol extension, scDblFinder had the highest mean AUPRC and AUROC on the same 16 datasets (PMID 34382023), which singletCode replicated [singletcode-2024](../records.md#singletcode-2024). pipeComp found that doublet calls and MAD-based outliers barely overlap, so a high-UMI cut-off is not a doublet filter [pipeComp](../records.md#pipecomp). The caveat for drug screens is in singletCode: on lineage-barcoded data with ground-truth singlets, mean AUPRC was 0.13-0.34 for all four detectors tested, lowest for scDblFinder [singletcode-2024](../records.md#singletcode-2024). RNA-based detectors find doublets of different cell types; a screen of one cell line produces mostly same-type doublets, which only tags or genotypes can see.

**QC thresholds.** The only independent benchmark of filtering stringency is pipeComp. Filtering against the whole population removed subpopulations with extreme but healthy properties such as large library size; clustering accuracy plateaued and then fell as stringency rose; its recommendation is 5 MADs, or 3 MADs on at least two metrics [pipeComp](../records.md#pipecomp). No independent benchmark of the adaptive QC tools below exists.

**Cell calling.** No independent benchmark of cell callers was found. The closest is the valiDrops paper, which ran seven callers on a 10x 8k PBMC sample: most called ~8,000 barcodes, dropkick and EmptyNN over 9,000 [valiDrops](../records.md#validrops).

## Published but not independently benchmarked

**Cell callers.** [CB2](../records.md#cb2) (above). dropkick learns a cell-versus-empty model per sample; on high-background simulations its specificity was 0.9999 against 0.9910 for the Cell Ranger v2 knee and 0.9838 for EmptyDrops, and on colorectal inDrop data it recovered more cells from rare populations while removing more dead cells [dropkick](../records.md#dropkick). SiftCell [SiftCell](../records.md#siftcell) and DIEM for nuclei [DIEM](../records.md#diem) target high-ambient data. CellBender also calls cells, but its calls have not been benchmarked separately [CellBender remove-background](../records.md#cellbender).

**Adaptive QC.**
- [miQC](../records.md#miqc) models mito fraction against genes detected as a mixture of intact and compromised cells. Besides the numbers above: pancreas, 7.8% removed against 45.5% at 10% mito; in six ovarian tumours 8.1-49.0% against 8.6-94.8%, though miQC removed more in one of the six. A fixed cut-off also removed 6.8% or 14.6% of the same library depending on the reference genome; miQC removed 10.6% and 8.8%.
- [ddqc](../records.md#ddqc) clusters first and applies MAD thresholds within clusters on genes, UMIs, mito and ribosomal fractions, keeping neutrophils and metabolically active parenchymal cells. miQC removed a cardiomyocyte cluster (median 15% mito) that ddqc kept.
- [MitoChontrol](../records.md#mitochontrol) (preprint) fits a mixture to mito fraction within each cluster. On HEK293 data with mito RNA inflated in 10% of cells, it removed the inflated subset and kept the rest, while ddqc kept many inflated cells; no removal counts are reported.
- [scQCenrich](../records.md#scqcenrich) adds intronic fraction, MALAT1 enrichment and stress features. On 10x mouse brain it removed 1,185 cells against 2,520 (Seurat-style), 1,801 (scater), 2,770 (miQC) and 1,968 (DropletQC).
- SampleQC fits a multivariate mixture across samples and cell types and was less likely to exclude rare types in simulations [SampleQC](../records.md#sampleqc).

**Damage detection.** [DropletQC](../records.md#dropletqc) uses the nuclear fraction, the share of reads from unspliced pre-mRNA. Damaged cells have lost cytoplasmic RNA, so they show high nuclear fraction and low UMIs; empty droplets show ambient-like, mostly spliced profiles. Among EmptyDrops calls it flagged 5.2-14.0% damaged cells in three tissues and almost none in PBMC; cryopreservation raised the damaged share from 4.1% to 13.8%. DamageDetective (CRAN, 2025) scores cells against simulated copies with cytoplasmic RNA removed; no paper was found.

**Ambient, gene-specific and DE-oriented.** scCDC corrects only the genes that cause contamination and reports that DecontX and CellBender under-correct those genes while SoupX and scAR over-correct housekeeping genes [scCDC](../records.md#sccdc). FastCAR targets differential expression; in asthma biopsies 214 genes were differentially expressed only after correction [FastCAR](../records.md#fastcar). Uncorrected ambient genes appeared among DEGs and enriched pathways in PBMC and fetal liver data [arora-2025](../records.md#arora-2025), and ambient RNA defined some previously annotated neuronal types in brain nuclei data [caglayan-2022](../records.md#caglayan-2022).

## Damage or biology: how to tell

**Mito fraction alone is a poor damage marker.** In sorted cells, dying (annexin V positive) cells had a median mito fraction of 3.13%, no higher than live cells (3.4%); only dead cells were high (29.9%) [oflanagan-2019](../records.md#oflanagan-2019). A mito cut-off therefore removes dead cells, not early apoptotic ones, and also removes healthy cells with a high baseline. Baselines vary by tissue [osorio-cai-2021](../records.md#osorio-cai-2021) and by cell line: median mito fraction across CCLE lines ran from 4% in glioblastoma to 14% in head and neck cancer, and higher mito fraction was associated with higher IC50, that is, drug resistance [mito-filter-resistant-cells](../records.md#mito-filter-resistant-cells). Filtering on mito in a drug screen can therefore remove the resistant cells selectively.

**Signals that separate them:**
- *Nuclear fraction.* A damaged cell has lost cytoplasm, so its remaining reads are nuclear (unspliced). DropletQC's damaged calls rose with apoptosis in staurosporine-treated HEK293 cells and had higher mito [DropletQC](../records.md#dropletqc). Requires BAM or spliced/unspliced counts.
- *Joint metrics.* Compromised cells combine high mito with few genes detected; healthy high-mito cells keep normal complexity [miQC](../records.md#miqc). MALAT1 and intronic fraction add nuclear-retention evidence [scQCenrich](../records.md#scqcenrich); MitoChontrol's removed cells were enriched for MALAT1 in 7 of 9 pancreatic tumour samples with significant differences [MitoChontrol](../records.md#mitochontrol).
- *Within-type comparison.* Thresholds computed within a cell type, cluster or sample avoid removing a whole type for its baseline [ddqc](../records.md#ddqc), [pipeComp](../records.md#pipecomp).
- *Ambient-like profile.* Droplets whose profile resembles the soup are empty or lysed, whatever their UMI count [EmptyDrops](../records.md#emptydrops).

**Stress is not damage.** Enzymatic dissociation at 37 C induces immediate-early and heat-shock genes in subsets of cells [van-den-brink-2017](../records.md#van-den-brink-2017); a 512-gene core set (FOS, FOSB, ATF3, heat-shock genes) was conserved across tumours and lines and largely avoided by cold protease at 6 C [oflanagan-2019](../records.md#oflanagan-2019). These cells are intact; score and model the signature rather than filtering them. Preparation choices also change who survives: cold dissociation of mouse kidney recovered 330 podocytes (2.78%) against 3 (0.03%) warm; cryopreserved suspensions gave ~30% fewer high-quality cells than fresh or methanol-fixed ones; single nuclei gave more genes per barcode (median 1,819 vs 981) but fewer immune cells (0.73% vs 6.03%) [denisenko-2020](../records.md#denisenko-2020). In tumours, cells and nuclei recovered the same types in different proportions (PMID 32405060). Fixation designs for pooled screens are covered in the design review ([Methanol fixation](../records.md#methanol-fixation), [10x Chromium Flex](../records.md#10x-flex)).

## Hypotheses and open questions

- [condition-aware-qc-audit](../records.md#condition-aware-qc-audit): drugs move the very metrics QC uses (global transcription, PMID 34159316; mito fraction, [mito-filter-resistant-cells](../records.md#mito-filter-resistant-cells)). A global fixed threshold then removes cells at a rate the drug sets, while thresholds computed per condition hide a toxic drug's damage. Thresholds per cell type and batch, plus a table of losses per condition and QC step, would expose the bias. No study has measured it.
- Open: no benchmark tests adaptive QC or cell calling on drug-treated cells. In a pooled lane the ambient profile mixes every condition, so a toxic drug's lysed cells contaminate all others in that lane; whether per-lane correction removes false drug-response genes in pseudobulk has not been tested. Nie et al.'s abstract and results disagree on which tool estimates contamination best.

## Recommendations for low-cell multiplexed drug screens

Ranked by expected gain per unit of effort. Gains are from the cited settings, not from drug screens, which have no direct benchmark.

1. **Audit losses per condition before choosing thresholds.** Tabulate cells removed by cell calling, each QC metric and doublet calling, for every drug and dose. Set thresholds per cell type and lane (or plate), never per drug. *Gain:* makes condition-dependent loss visible; no measured number. *Data:* filtered matrix, tag assignments. [condition-aware-qc-audit](../records.md#condition-aware-qc-audit)
2. **Replace fixed mito and gene cut-offs with lenient adaptive ones.** Use 5 MADs within cell type and lane [MAD-based adaptive QC thresholds](../records.md#mad-adaptive-qc), or a mixture model ([miQC](../records.md#miqc), [MitoChontrol](../records.md#mitochontrol)) where mito is high at baseline, as in many cancer lines. *Gain:* 41% vs 19% removed [miQC](../records.md#miqc); 69.4% vs 95.4% retained [ddqc](../records.md#ddqc). *Data:* filtered matrix.
3. **Re-call cells from the raw matrix.** Run `emptyDrops` (lower bound 100) on the raw matrix rather than trusting the 500-UMI candidate floor of Cell Ranger-style calling; consider [CB2](../records.md#cb2); check rescued barcodes with nuclear fraction and tag strength. *Gain:* 4-81% more cells in tissue data, self-reported [CB2](../records.md#cb2). *Data:* raw matrix; tag counts help [Tag-guided rescue of low-RNA cells](../records.md#tag-guided-cell-rescue).
4. **Use nuclear fraction, not mito alone, to call damage.** Run [DropletQC](../records.md#dropletqc) or [scQCenrich](../records.md#scqcenrich); remove cells flagged damaged by both nuclear fraction and low complexity; keep high-mito cells with normal complexity. Report damaged share per condition. *Gain:* keeps intact high-mito cells; flags damage that mito misses in early apoptosis. *Data:* BAM or spliced/unspliced counts.
5. **Correct ambient RNA per lane before differential expression.** [CellBender remove-background](../records.md#cellbender) with a raw matrix and GPU, [DecontX](../records.md#decontx) with a filtered matrix only, [SoupX](../records.md#soupx) reduced mode as the conservative option when contamination is low. Check that control-only genes do not appear in treated cells after correction. *Gain:* Slc34a1 leakage 60% to 7% (CellBender) [janssen-2023](../records.md#janssen-2023); risk of over-correction on clean samples [cargnelli-2026](../records.md#cargnelli-2026). *Data:* raw matrix (CellBender, SoupX full mode).
6. **Do not drop cells on RNA doublet scores or high-UMI caps alone.** Use tags for cross-sample doublets (demultiplexing review); run [scDblFinder](../records.md#scdblfinder) per lane only where cell types are mixed. *Gain:* avoids losing large healthy cells [pipeComp](../records.md#pipecomp); in single-line screens RNA detectors reach mean AUPRC of only 0.13-0.34 [singletcode-2024](../records.md#singletcode-2024). *Data:* filtered matrix, tag counts.
7. **At the bench:** remove dead and apoptotic cells before loading (PMID 31603610), use cold dissociation for tissue [oflanagan-2019](../records.md#oflanagan-2019), and prefer fixation or nuclei when samples must wait [denisenko-2020](../records.md#denisenko-2020).
